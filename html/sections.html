The Reading & Writing Sections

Reading: Information & Ideas

Central Ideas These questions ask you to identify the main point, primary purpose, or central theme of a passage. They focus on the overall message rather than specific details. 2-3 Questions
Details These questions require you to identify the evidence in a passage that best supports a claim, answer, or conclusion. You'll need to evaluate which textual details provide the strongest support. 2-3 Questions
Inferences These questions ask you to draw logical conclusions based on what the passage suggests rather than explicitly states. The best answer is supported by the text, even if it isn't directly stated. 2-3 Questions
Command of Evidence: Textual These questions require you to identify the evidence in a passage that best supports a claim, answer, or conclusion. You'll need to evaluate which textual details provide the strongest support. 2-3 Questions
Command of Evidence: Quantitative These questions ask you to interpret information from tables, graphs, or charts and determine how it relates to the accompanying text. You'll need to combine quantitative data with reading comprehension to answer correctly. 1-2 Questions

Reading: Craft & Structure

Words in Context These questions ask you to determine the meaning of a word or phrase as it is used in the passage. Focus on how the surrounding context shapes the word's meaning rather than its dictionary definition. 2-3 Questions
Text Purpose These questions ask why the author included a particular sentence, paragraph, or passage. You'll need to identify the author's purpose, such as introducing an idea, providing evidence, or making a comparison. 1-2 Questions
Text Structure These questions test your understanding of how a passage is organized and how its parts work together. You may be asked to identify the function of a sentence or explain how the author develops an idea. 1-2 Questions
Cross-Text Connections These questions present two related passages and ask you to compare their ideas, perspectives, or conclusions. You'll need to determine how the texts relate to one another using evidence from both. 1-2 Questions

Writing: Standard English Conventions

Boundaries These questions test your ability to correctly separate ideas using punctuation. You'll need to choose the appropriate periods, commas, semicolons, colons, or dashes to create clear and grammatically correct sentences. 3-5 Questions
Form, Structure, & Sense These questions assess your understanding of grammar and sentence construction. You may be asked to choose the correct verb form, pronoun, modifier, or word that best fits the sentence's meaning and follows the rules of standard English. 2-4 Questions

Writing: Expression of Ideas

Transitions These questions ask you to choose the transition word or phrase that best connects ideas within or between sentences. You'll need to determine the logical relationship, such as addition, contrast, cause and effect, or sequence. 2-3 Questions
Rhetorical Synthesis These questions ask you to select the answer that best achieves a specific writing goal using provided notes or information. You'll need to identify the choice that is most relevant, concise, and effective for the stated purpose. 1-3 Questions

Math

Algebra

Linear Equations One Variable These questions ask you to solve equations containing a single variable. You may need to isolate the variable by simplifying expressions, combining like terms, or working with fractions and decimals. 2-3 Questions
Linear Equations Two Variables These questions involve equations with two variables, typically written in the form y = mx + b or Ax + By = C. You may be asked to find a missing value, determine whether a point satisfies the equation, or interpret the relationship between the variables. 1-2 Questions
Linear Functions These questions test your understanding of linear functions and their graphs. You may need to identify or interpret the slope, y-intercept, rate of change, function values, or write an equation from a graph, table, or real-world scenario. 2-3 Questions
Linear Inequalities These questions ask you to solve or interpret linear inequalities. You may need to find the solution set, graph an inequality, or determine which values satisfy a given condition. 1-2 Questions
Systems of Linear Equations These questions require you to solve systems of two linear equations using algebraic or graphical methods. You may be asked to find the point of intersection or determine whether a system has one, none, or infinitely many solutions. 2-3 Questions
Linear Word Problems These questions involve applying linear equations and functions to real-world situations. You'll need to translate a scenario into a mathematical model and use it to solve for an unknown quantity or interpret the results. 1-2 Questions

Advanced Math

Quadratic Equations & Functions These questions focus on analyzing and solving quadratic equations and functions. You may be asked to find solutions (roots, zeros), identify the vertex, convert between standard and vertex form, or interpret the graph of a parabola. 2-3 Questions
Exponential Equations & Functions These questions test your understanding of exponential growth and decay. You may need to evaluate exponential expressions, interpret graphs, or model real-world situations involving increasing or decreasing quantities. 1-2 Questions
Polynomial Functions These questions involve polynomial equations and functions of varying degrees. You may be asked to solve polynomial equations, identify key features of graphs, determine end behavior, or analyze the relationship between factors and zeros. 2-3 Questions
Rational Functions These questions assess your understanding of functions involving rational expressions. You may need to identify intercepts, determine asymptotes, simplify rational expressions, or analyze the behavior of a graph. 1-2 Questions
Equivalent Expressions These questions ask you to rewrite algebraic expressions without changing their value. You'll use techniques such as factoring, expanding, combining like terms, or applying exponent rules to produce an equivalent expression. 2-3 Questions
Transformations These questions ask you to analyze how changes to an equation affect the graph of a function. You'll need to recognize shifts, reflections, stretches, compressions, and other transformations of linear, quadratic, exponential, or other function types. 1-2 Questions

Geometry

Area & Volume These questions test your ability to calculate and interpret measurements of two- and three-dimensional figures. You may need to find the area, surface area, or volume of shapes such as rectangles, triangles, circles, cylinders, cones, and other geometric solids. 2-3 Questions
Lines, Angles, & Triangles These questions focus on relationships between geometric figures, including angle relationships, parallel lines, and properties of triangles. You may need to use angle rules, the Pythagorean theorem, similarity, congruence, or special triangle properties to solve problems. 3-4 Questions
Circles These questions involve properties of circles, including radius, diameter, circumference, area, arcs, and angles. You may need to analyze circle equations, use circle theorems, or interpret relationships between angles and arcs. 1-2 Questions
Trigonometry These questions test your understanding of right-triangle trigonometry and relationships between angles and side lengths. You may need to use sine, cosine, and tangent to solve for missing measurements or apply trigonometric concepts to real-world situations. 1-2 Questions

Problem Solving & Data Analysis

Ratios, Rates, & Proportions These questions test your ability to compare quantities and solve problems involving proportional relationships. You may need to set up ratios, calculate unit rates, convert between units, or use proportions to find unknown values in real-world scenarios. 2-3 Questions
Percentages These questions involve calculating and interpreting percent changes, increases, decreases, and comparisons. You may need to apply percentages to real-world situations involving discounts, growth, taxes, interest, or data analysis. 1-2 Questions
Distribution & Center of Spread These questions focus on analyzing data sets and understanding how values are distributed. You may be asked to calculate or interpret measures of center (mean, median), measures of spread (range, standard deviation), and how changes in data affect these values. 1-2 Questions
Two-Variable Data & Scatterplots These questions involve analyzing relationships between two variables using tables, graphs, and scatterplots. You may need to identify trends, interpret correlation, estimate values, or determine whether a linear model appropriately represents the data. 1-2 Questions
Probability These questions test your ability to calculate and interpret the likelihood of events. You may need to find probabilities of single or combined events, use probability models, or analyze situations involving random samples and outcomes. 1-2 Questions
Data Inferences & Margin of Error These questions ask you to draw conclusions from data collected through surveys or experiments. You may need to interpret confidence intervals, understand margin of error, and determine how sample size or sampling methods affect the reliability of results. 1-2 Questions
Evaluating Statistical Claims These questions test your ability to assess whether a conclusion is supported by data. You may need to identify appropriate evidence, evaluate study designs, recognize potential bias, and determine whether a claim can reasonably be made based on the information provided. 1-2 Questions
